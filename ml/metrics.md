## Cleaning decisions

Loaded 9,160 rows × 20 columns from leads.csv

**Class balance**: 634 converted / 9,160 = 6.92%
A model predicting 'nobody converts' scores 93.08% accuracy and creates zero value. This is why the metric is PR-AUC (see below).

**Leak check — `token_amount_received_pkr`**: token = 0 → 0 of 8,441 converted; token > 0 → 634 of 719 converted.
A received token *is* the booking. Dropped — it is always zero when a fresh lead actually needs scoring.

**`bedrooms` missingness**: 3,602 missing (39.3%); Plot + Commercial Shop rows = 3,602. Identical sets: True.
Not missing data — structurally inapplicable. Imputing a median bedroom count into a plot of land invents a fact, so it becomes an explicit `not_applicable` category instead.

**Deduplicated** on `crm_record_hash`: 9,160 → 9,000 (160 duplicate rows removed).
**Normalised `city`**: 21 spellings → 9 canonical cities.
**Derived from `created_at`**: hour_of_day, day_of_week, month — a 2 a.m. portal form is a different animal from a 3 p.m. walk-in.
**Dropped identifiers**: `lead_id`, `crm_record_hash`.

## Why PR-AUC

- **Accuracy is useless here.** 93.04% is achievable by predicting that nobody ever converts.
- **ROC-AUC flatters.** With ~7,900 negatives in the deduplicated set, a large absolute number of false positives barely moves the false-positive *rate*, so ROC-AUC stays respectable even when the top of the ranking is mostly noise.
- **PR-AUC scores only the positive class**, and its no-skill baseline is the prevalence itself (0.0696) — the number arrives with its own "did this beat nothing?" reference.
- **Precision@200 is the business number.** The deliverable is a ranked call list and the team can work about 200 leads a cycle.

Threshold 0.5 is reported for completeness and is the wrong operating point: for a ranking task the cut should come from call capacity, not a default.
